# ADR: One served surface serves many Minds

Status: proposed — one served surface serves many Minds, reached by Mind id, in two stages with a recorded fallback; the owner settled it on 2026-11-25 and stated that SPOKE 0.19.0 will carry the three SPOKE-side items the second stage needs; the surface still serves one collection
Date: 2026-11-25
Class: architecture
Supersedes: `2026-11-21-principal-routing.md` — its principal-keyed route map, once this is implemented
Related: `2026-11-21-spoke-017-serving-cohort.md`, `2026-11-25-mind-is-one-memory-space.md`, `.agents/specs/architecture/serving-principal-routing.md`, `.agents/specs/architecture/serving-seam.md`

## Problem

A served surface is assembled around one knowledge collection. `ServeFaces.scope_id` is "the one configured route", `ServeFaces.knowledge` is "the collection that route serves", and `ServeFaces.admission` maps each peer to one scope (`crates/mindcore-runtime/src/lib.rs`). `port.knowledge.get`, `port.knowledge.put` and the list operation read and write that collection for every admitted peer, `history.*` serves the same store, and `knowledge.adopt` writes it. The two routed workflows reach a collection through `ServeFaces.workflow_routes`, a host-configured map from principal id to collection that is fixed for the surface's lifetime (`.agents/specs/architecture/serving-principal-routing.md`).

So one process cannot let a peer operate several Minds or list them: a host that wants more than one Mind assembles one surface per Mind. A peer has no wire value that names a Mind; the workflow tools name memory coordinates, and every `deliberate` names its carrier by `holder` (`crates/mindcore-runtime/src/tools.rs`). And because reach is host configuration, a Mind created after assembly is unreachable until the surface is rebuilt.

## Proposal

One served surface serves many Minds, reached by Mind id. This design was proposed to SPOKE for evaluation (`https://github.com/42ch-dev/spoke/issues/222`); the owner stated on 2026-11-25 that SPOKE settled it, that SPOKE 0.19.0 will carry A1, A2 and A3, and that the caller names the `space_id`. Whether the fallback named below is taken remains the owner's direct decision.

The Mind id rides the selectors SPOKE requests already carry: `space_id` on a memory request and `scope_id` on a knowledge `Scope`. A Mind's id is its space id (`2026-11-25-mind-is-one-memory-space.md`), so no new wire member is needed.

**Resolution and admission.** The wire never names a collection. For each call Mindcore resolves the Mind id in one registry lookup to a space, a collection and a carrier, admits the (principal, Mind) pair, and only then binds the collection to a port.

- Memory: a live grant covering the operation, in that space. This is today's rule.
- Knowledge, history and adoption: a live `knowledge_read` or `knowledge_write` grant in the Mind's space, answered as that Mind's admitted `Scope`.
- Workflow tools, each where it reaches the adapter (`crates/mindcore-spoke/src/workflows.rs`): `recall_context` checks `recall`, and `knowledge_read` only to fill its knowledge slot. `absorb` checks `capture`, `review` to read the candidate and `recall` for its page, then `review` with `records_write` on one grant to review, `records_write` to update and `review` to dismiss. `deliberate` checks `recall` over a `subject_all` page, `knowledge_read` and `knowledge_write`.

**Refuse, do not filter.** Kept (`docs/concepts.md`). Each call admits one Mind, and the handler binds a port to the resolved collection, so no result mixes two Minds. Mind admission precedes every existence check: an unknown Mind id and a Mind the peer holds no grant for answer one `NOT_AUTHORIZED`, with no `details`, before any subject, entry or carrier is read. Today the memory path answers `NOT_FOUND` for an unknown space and `NOT_AUTHORIZED` for an ungranted one (`crates/mindcore-spoke/src/admission.rs`, `space` and `refuse_coverage`), which lets a peer tell whether a Mind exists.

**A frozen Mind.** A Mind that is not active is frozen (`2026-11-25-mind-is-one-memory-space.md`), and it answers by SPOKE's freeze semantics as the adapter applies them. A caller without a grant in its space is refused as an unknown Mind is, because the grant check comes before the freeze check (`crates/mindcore-spoke/src/admission.rs`, `authorize` and `gate`). A caller with a grant reads. `gate` answers `SpaceFrozen` only to an operation that is freeze-gated and outside the maintenance allowlist, which SPOKE defines, and serves the operations that are not freeze-gated (`crates/mindcore-spoke/src/admission.rs`, `gate`). Freeze-gated operations include `memory.capture`, `memory.candidates.review`, `memory.records.update` and `memory.profile.update` (`crates/mindcore-spoke/src/content.rs`, `capture_body`, `review_body`, `update_record_body`, `update_profile_body`), and `memory.subjects.ensure` and `memory.grants.grant` (`crates/mindcore-spoke/src/control.rs`, `ensure_subject_body`, `grant_body`).

**Stage 1, no SPOKE change.** It needs no SPOKE change and exposes Mind-level convenience operations only: the space-level capabilities beneath them are an advanced surface that may be exposed later. It composes none of the operations that are not Mind-routed: until each of `port.knowledge.*`, `history.*` and `knowledge.adopt` is served per Mind, the surface does not compose it, because every operation is to hang off the Mind entity (owner, 2026-11-25).

- `memory.spaces.list` lists Minds.
- The memory family and the three workflow tools name a Mind through the `space_id` they already carry; no member is added and none is renamed. In `recall_context` and `absorb` that `space_id` sits inside the pinned SPOKE request, and only `deliberate` carries `space_id` and `holder` at the top level (`crates/mindcore-runtime/src/tools.rs`, the argument structs), so another selector name would be a SPOKE change, which Stage 1 does not make.
- The workflow tools' one argument change is that `deliberate` loses `holder`, because the Mind's record names the carrier. The facade's `deliberate` takes no `subject_id` (owner, 2026-11-25): the grant re-check and the unsettled-intent check move to the space, and both are verified against the code when the facade is prepared (`2026-11-25-three-delivery-forms.md`).
- A new `tools.mindcore.read_cognition` takes `space_id`, as the memory operations do, and returns that Mind's current judgment and derived `MindState`.
- One `NOT_AUTHORIZED` answers an unknown or unadmitted Mind before any existence check, a frozen Mind included.

**Stage 2, three SPOKE-side items.** A1 is request-scoped admission: the admission callback receives the `scope_id` a request declares. It is additive and source-compatible. A2 is an optional `scope` on `port.knowledge.get` and `port.knowledge.put`, with a negotiable marker saying the responder honours it; it is a wire change, so a new SPOKE cohort. A3 is `memory.spaces.create`, the operation a SPOKE peer creates a Mind through. A1 and A2 turn on knowledge, history and adoption per Mind; until both exist, those three operations keep one collection per peer and are documented as not Mind-routed. A3 turns on a peer's creation of a Mind. SPOKE 0.19.0 will carry A1, A2 and A3, and the caller names the `space_id` (owner, 2026-11-25).

**Creating a Mind.** A library caller creates a Mind through `create`, `list` and `get` in `crates/mindcore-runtime`, which need no SPOKE change and are built; the facade (`2026-11-25-three-delivery-forms.md`) builds on them. A SPOKE peer creates one through `memory.spaces.create`, which is A3 of Stage 2 and which SPOKE 0.19.0 will carry. On a Mindcore host both run the same create and write the default grant set (`2026-11-25-create-default-grant-set.md`). Until this repository pins that cohort, a peer operates only Minds created through the library.

**The fallback.** Design T is the fallback: every Mind-scoped operation is a `tools.mindcore.*` tool whose argument object starts with `mind_id`, with no SPOKE change. SPOKE and Mindcore have the same owner, so whether the fallback is taken is the owner's direct decision, and it needs no trigger. `mind_id` is there a tool argument of Design T's own, not a renamed SPOKE member: every operation becomes a new tool, and the argument object is that tool's to define.

**Records and pages this changes.** `ServeFaces.workflow_routes` is replaced by the registry's Mind-to-collection row, which retires the route `2026-11-21-principal-routing.md` records. Its rules that outlive the map hold per Mind: the wire never names a collection, a refusal carries no target details, and a route never substitutes for a live grant. `.agents/specs/architecture/serving-principal-routing.md` gains the request's Mind id as a route input and loses the map, and the knowledge page `.agents/knowledge/architecture-patterns/serving-principal-routing.md` describes that map and goes stale with it. `.agents/specs/architecture/serving-seam.md` changes the tool argument table — the `deliberate` row loses `holder`, the `recall_context` and `absorb` rows keep their request types, and one row is added for `tools.mindcore.read_cognition` — and, in Stage 2, the half of the knowledge contract that says `get` and `put` carry no wire scope. In `2026-11-21-spoke-017-serving-cohort.md` the tool list and the routing sentence are brought up to date. `docs/serving.md` loses its `workflow_routes` row, and `docs/concepts.md` states the closed existence gap.

## Alternatives considered

- **Design T: every Mind-scoped operation a `tools.mindcore.*` tool, no SPOKE change.** Rejected as the primary route and kept as the fallback. It restates `port.knowledge.*`, `history.*` and `knowledge.adopt` as six product tools, which is a second operation surface inside the tool namespace and reverses the migration that replaced `tools.mindcore.adopt` with `knowledge.adopt` (`.agents/specs/architecture/serving-seam.md`). The surface would still carry the per-peer `port.knowledge.*` path beside those tools, and a tool's arguments pass outside SPOKE's scope and cognition gates. The chosen route rides selectors SPOKE already has and keeps the peer on standard operations.
- **One responder per Mind, no SPOKE change.** Rejected: it is not one surface serving many Minds. Each Mind gets its own surface, manifest, handler set and one signed session per peer, a peer has no way to list Minds, and the hello manifest repeats for every (peer, Mind) pair. Whether an unadmitted Mind's address differs from an unknown one is left to the host's transport.
- **One collection, with Minds separated per entry, no SPOKE change.** Rejected: SPOKE's scope predicate has no entry-ownership term and an admitted scope is fixed per peer, so two peers could not be held to different Minds. It also gives up the one-collection-per-Mind binding of `2026-11-25-mind-is-one-memory-space.md`.
- **Ship no peer form until the SPOKE-side items exist.** Rejected: it ties a first release to a SPOKE release Mindcore does not control. Stage 1 needs no SPOKE change and gives a peer per-Mind memory and cognition; what it costs is that knowledge, history and adoption stay one collection per peer until Stage 2. The third round's release timing makes a release without the peer capabilities impossible until SPOKE 0.19.0; that is a release decision and does not adopt this alternative.

## Acceptance criteria

- One served surface serves more than one Mind. A call naming a Mind resolves its space, knowledge collection and carrier through the host's admission, and a peer lists and operates only the Minds it is admitted to.
- A signed loopback test runs two Minds and two peers and asserts that an unadmitted Mind answers the same refusal as an absent one, and that adoption still writes only the surface's configured destination.
- A peer with no Mind is refused without target details.
- Stage 1 is served on the pinned SPOKE cohort: `memory.spaces.list` lists Minds, the memory family and the three workflow tools name a Mind through the `space_id` they already carry with no member added or renamed, `tools.mindcore.deliberate` takes no `holder`, and `tools.mindcore.read_cognition`, taking `space_id`, returns current judgment and `MindState`.
- A recall, and every other memory read that looks a space up, answers one refusal with one code and no target details for an unknown Mind id and a Mind the caller holds no grant for, a frozen Mind included; one test pins these cases on that answer. On a frozen Mind a granted caller is served every operation that is not freeze-gated, and a freeze-gated operation outside the maintenance allowlist answers `SpaceFrozen`; one test pins both.
- `.agents/specs/architecture/serving-principal-routing.md` and `.agents/specs/architecture/serving-seam.md` are updated in the same change, and the operations that are not Mind-routed are documented as such.

## Risks

- **Before SPOKE 0.19.0 a peer gets per-Mind memory and cognition only.** `port.knowledge.*`, `history.*` and `knowledge.adopt` answer from the surface's one collection, which no Mind owns. No release ships Stage 1 alone: the first Mind-centric release follows SPOKE 0.19.0 and ships Stage 1 and Stage 2 as one minor (owner, 2026-11-25).
- **The argument change breaks existing `deliberate` callers.** `holder` is a required member of today's `deliberate` arguments and the argument object is closed (`crates/mindcore-runtime/src/tools.rs`), so a peer built on today's `deliberate` fails after the change. `recall_context` and `absorb` arguments do not change. It is a Mindcore release change, not a SPOKE one.
- **A2 without its marker misroutes silently.** A responder that ignores the new `scope` drops it, and the read lands in the peer's default collection. The marker is part of the ask and is not optional for Mindcore.
- **Admission stays synchronous and bounded.** The registry lookup behind a Mind admission runs under the responder's serve budget and must fit inside it.
- **Closing the existence gap changes answers tests may pin.** The memory path answers `NOT_FOUND` for an unknown space today (`crates/mindcore-spoke/src/admission.rs`), and the adapter tests that pin it change with it.
- **Settled: a many-Mind surface composes none of the operations that are not Mind-routed.** Until each of `port.knowledge.*`, `history.*` and `knowledge.adopt` is served per Mind, the surface does not compose it, because every operation is to hang off the Mind entity (owner, 2026-11-25).
- **Undecided: the grant rule for a knowledge put that carries cognition records.** `port.knowledge.put` appends `modules.cognition.records` on the admitted scope alone (`crates/mindcore-sqlite/src/knowledge.rs`, `served_put`), where `deliberate` requires `knowledge_write` (`crates/mindcore-spoke/src/workflows.rs`). The asymmetry exists today, and routing knowledge per Mind would carry it to every admitted Mind. The rule is settled before knowledge is routed per Mind.
- **A3 is not in the pinned SPOKE cohort yet.** `memory.spaces.create` does not exist in 0.18.1, and SPOKE 0.19.0 will carry it (owner, 2026-11-25). Until this repository pins that cohort, a peer can operate Minds and cannot create one. On a Mindcore host the operation runs the same create as the library, `mindcore_sqlite::minds::create`, which borrows the caller's transaction.
- **Design T, if taken, costs more than Stage 2**: eight new tools, six of which restate standard operations, and a second knowledge path on the surface.
